

# Introduction

This document is intended to help people follow the work of the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) from a public policy perspective. We have tried to indicate which activities might be of policy interest.

This is not the only curated resource for people interested in IETF activities for reasons beyond their technical aspects. [Article 19 produced a web page](https://almanac.article19.org/orgs/3_ietf.html) with an overview of working groups whose work involves human rights considerations. The IETF also publishes lists of new topics at its meetings. We expect the list for IETF 127 to appear in the blog post [Suggested IETF 127 Sessions for Getting Familiar with New Topics](https://www.ietf.org/blog/ietf127-new-topics/).

## Curation

The information in this document is curated. The editor has made a subjective judgment about which work might have public policy implications.

Work in the IETF may not have an immediate impact on public policy. Given the building-block nature of IETF specifications, their effects may become apparent only after they have been deployed on the Internet as part of larger systems or services. This makes potential public policy impacts harder to identify.

Our approach is as follows:
* We include all Birds of a Feather (BoF) sessions organized for a meeting. Even if a BoF does not appear to meet any of the criteria loosely described above, BoF sessions are useful places to observe issues as they emerge.
* We list [new working groups](./IETF/NewWG.md) formed since the last meeting, with a short description or a link to relevant information elsewhere in this document.
* For other working groups, we include those whose initial descriptions suggest public policy implications. We also include groups where we perceive changes in control or business relationships, or changes in visibility into certain aspects of Internet traffic. This part of the curation is more intuitive and subjective.

We do not mention working groups that are clearly focused on technical building blocks and have no identifiable public policy angles. For the working groups we do mention, we have tried to use keywords to indicate where public policy issues might arise—even if only when the technical building blocks are used to design and implement systems.

This is a living document. We try to update it shortly before each IETF meeting, but may also publish new versions between meetings.

Finally, for transparency, the editor uses artificial intelligence during editing, mainly for style and clarity improvements. The editor is responsible for the assessments of what groups to include, initial writing, and editorial decisions.
