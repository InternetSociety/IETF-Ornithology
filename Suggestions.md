# Content suggestions for `src/Introduction.md`

These are editorial suggestions only; no changes to the introduction have been made.

1. **Make the intended audience more concrete.** The opening says the document is for people following IETF work “from a public policy perspective.” Consider briefly naming likely readers and giving examples of the policy questions in scope (for example, privacy, human rights, competition, or governance), while making clear that the list is illustrative.

2. **Clarify what “policy implications” means in the curation criteria.** The criteria are candidly described as subjective, but a short explanation or a few examples of what leads a working group to be included could help readers understand how to interpret the selections and improve consistency between updates.

3. **Distinguish possible implications from established effects.** Since the introduction notes that impacts may emerge only after deployment, consider explicitly saying that inclusion flags topics worth watching; it does not assert that a policy effect will occur or endorse a particular outcome.

4. **Explain the limits of omission.** The text says some technically focused working groups are not mentioned. Consider adding that omission should not be read as a judgment that their work can never have policy relevance, especially as specifications are combined or deployed in new contexts.

5. **Keep meeting-specific references resilient over time.** The reference to IETF 127 and its new-topics blog post is useful, but it can become stale as the document is updated. Consider making the meeting-specific reference clearly date-scoped, or pointing readers to a maintained index/current-meeting resource when one is available.

6. **Add a brief note about evidence and uncertainty.** If consistent with the editorial process, consider stating whether entries are based on working-group charters, meeting materials, drafts, or other sources, and how tentative or evolving work is represented. This would help readers assess the strength and currency of each entry.

7. **Be slightly more specific about AI’s role, if useful and accurate.** The closing disclosure is transparent. A short clarification of what AI assists with—and what remains the editor’s responsibility, such as source checking and final selection—could make the disclosure more informative without overstating the role of the tool.
